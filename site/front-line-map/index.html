Ukraine front line map

An area-level Ukraine war map of assessed control. It is delayed and approximate, and it is not a picture of where units are.

Estimates, not real-time intelligence. Everything here comes from official statements and open-source (OSINT) reporting, is delayed by hours to days, and is attributed to its source. Figures are reported claims and may be revised.
No live military positions. This site does not publish unit locations, troop movements or anything that could help target people. It only links to published reporting and aggregates official statistics after the fact.

What this map shows

Light blue is assessed as Ukrainian-controlled, amber as contested, and red as Russian-controlled. The shapes are populated-place cells that share a status, dissolved together and simplified so the file stays small. A pocket of contested ground can be only a few places, so it may look small next to the rest of the country.

How the map is made

Each build reads the latest VIINA 2.0 control table, keeps that day’s status for every populated place, and joins it to the VIINA tessellation. VIINA 2.0 (Zhukov & Ayers) is available under the Open Database License (ODbL). The derived control.geojson is published under the ODbL as well. If the download fails, the previous layer stays up. Nothing here is a movement arrow or a unit location.

Front line map FAQ

Is this a live Ukraine war map?

No. This Ukraine front line map is area-level and delayed by days. It does not show military units, troop movements, or a live front line.

What does the Ukraine front line map show?

It shows three assessed areas: Ukrainian-controlled, contested, and Russian-controlled. Populated-place statuses are merged into those areas and then simplified. Boundaries are approximate.

Where does the Ukraine control data come from?

VIINA 2.0 by Yuri Zhukov and Natalie Ayers, shared under the Open Database License (ODbL). The simplified GeoJSON on this page is published under the ODbL as well.
